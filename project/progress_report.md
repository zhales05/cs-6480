There's no ready-made dataset of podcast episodes with ads already labeled, so everything has to be built from scratch. Podcasts are easy enough to download, but they vary a lot in format and style, and some use dynamically inserted ads that change every time you pull the episode, making it tough to even pin down a stable dataset.
Labeling is where the real time sink hits. You have to actually listen through each episode to mark where ads start and stop, and it's not always obvious. Host-read ads blend right into the conversation — a host might slide from a personal story into a sponsor plug without any clear transition. That subjectivity makes it hard to label consistently.
Audio quality is all over the place too. Some shows are studio-polished, others sound like they were recorded in a closet. Pre-produced ads inserted by a network can sound completely different from the host reading copy in their own voice. All that variation muddies the signal the model is trying to learn.
And with only a few weeks on the clock, there's a hard limit on how much data one person can realistically label. A dozen or so episodes might only give you 40–60 ad segments total, which is pretty thin for training a deep learning model. That pushes you toward simpler architectures or leaning on pre-trained models, which comes with its own trade-offs.
